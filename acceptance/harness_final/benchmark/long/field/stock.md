**Publish the tenfold claim for total volume, not for yield per square mile.** During May 1–31, 2023, the Potomac at Little Falls carried **424.45 times** as much water as the Northwest Branch Anacostia near Hyattsville. Its drainage area was about 234 times as large, so its gauged volume per square mile was only **1.81 times** that of the Northwest Branch.

| USGS gauge | May volume (billion L) | Drainage area (mi²) | May yield (million L/mi²) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Potomac River at Little Falls Pump Station (01646500) | 840.105 | 11,560 | 72.67 |
| Northwest Branch Anacostia River near Hyattsville (01651000) | 1.97928 | 49.4 | 40.07 |

Both gauges had approved USGS daily mean discharge values on **all 31 dates**, with no gaps. Each monthly volume is the sum of those daily means multiplied by 86,400 seconds per day and converted to liters. Yield is that volume divided by the station inventory’s reported drainage area (`drain_area_va`).

**Neither verdict changes under the desk’s ±5% flow scenario.** With drainage areas fixed, lowering Potomac flow by 5% and raising Northwest Branch flow by 5% gives the minimum ratio; reversing the changes gives the maximum. The total-volume ratio ranges from **384.03 to 469.13**, always above ten. The per-square-mile ratio ranges from **1.641 to 2.005**, always below ten. These are sensitivity bounds, not USGS error estimates.
