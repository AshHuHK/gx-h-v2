# Closing decision

The named purified-enzyme assays, archive records and duplicate-sequence evidence justify the editor's wording and identify its experimental limits.

The colleague traced the preparations through the primary article and followed the attribution into the S288C archive. Experimental procedures §§4.2–4.3 identify the plasmid templates, expression host and glucose-release assay. Table 2 and Fig. 1 report isomaltose hydrolysis by Ima1p, Ima2p, Ima3p and Ima5p, corresponding to P53051, Q08295, P0CW40 and P40884. Table 3 reports fits for individual preparations. The Introduction, cloning list and archive sequence digests identify P0CW41 as encoding the same protein as P0CW40, with IMA3 supplying the assayed construct. The archive fields exact_reaction, parents and assay_coverage distinguish enzyme-class links from direct reaction links, named isomaltose entries from other or unresolved substrates, and individual from jointly indexed accessions. The shared P53051/Q08295 affinity entry counts once. The record examples and aggregate coverage leave their strain, preparation, source publication and independence undetermined without further provenance. Empty links leave activity undetermined. Native whole-cell hydrolysis, locus expression and contributions in yeast or dough lie outside these observations. The source locations and attribution limits settle the requested editorial wording.

## Limitations

- The strain, preparation and publication behind each indexed entry, the substrates left unnamed, and the independent measurements behind a jointly indexed record remain undetermined from the lookup.
- The IMA4-encoded protein was represented by the identical IMA3 sequence. Separate IMA4 activity and its relative contribution in the native strain remain unknown.
- Native whole-cell S288C hydrolysis, native expression of each locus, and the contribution of each locus in yeast or dough remain unmeasured here.
