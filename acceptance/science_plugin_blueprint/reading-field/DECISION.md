# Closing decision

The reported correlations and their independently checked definitions answer the question. The study supports descriptive comparisons of satellite-detected lighting and gives insufficient grounds for ranking countries by economic growth from their lighting changes.

Figure 4 directly tests the proposed ordering across 134 eligible countries. For changes from 2012 to 2016, the reported Spearman correlation with real GDP growth was 0.17 for the change in nationally summed radiance (P = 0.05) and 0.24 for the change in lit area (P = 0.006). Both indicate weak agreement between country rankings. The director can compare changes in detected lighting and can report that GDP and light levels were strongly associated across countries, with correlations of 0.85 for national totals and 0.76 per capita (both P ≪ 0.001). The median increases of 15% in radiance and 13% in GDP summarize the distributions separately. Independent review of the partial module’s two extractions against the Results, Figure 4 caption and Methods confirmed these assignments and resolved the outstanding interpretation. All are observations from one paper.

## Limitations

- The paper gives no region-specific ranking accuracy or calibration for estimating an individual country’s GDP growth.
- The size of any country-specific bias from the radiance threshold, transient fires, or changes in lamp spectra is unknown, as is performance over a longer interval.
